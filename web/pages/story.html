9 · The extension

In a normal runoff, nearly every urna closes on time

Brazil votes in sections: one room, one electronic voting machine (urna), a few hundred voters. When a section closes, its urna prints a boletim de urna with the result and the exact open and close times.

The law lets every voter who is in line at 17:00 vote, so many urnas close a few minutes late. In the 2022 runoff only — of — sections were still open 49 minutes after the close.

On 25 October an STF order at 17:03 extended voting to 19:00. The TSE told the sections still open at 17:49. Sections that had closed did not reopen.

10 · Queue or court order

A late close does not mean late votes

The urna also keeps a log with the time of every vote. Each dot here is one real vote from one urna in Cruzeiro do Sul, Acre. The bar is the moment it closed.

Top: round 1. People in line at 17:00 kept voting with no break, then the urna closed. That is a queue, and it is legal in every election.

Bottom: the same urna in the runoff. The last vote was before 17:00. The urna stayed open until 19:00, and nobody came. Its close time says "late", but it had no late votes. Only votes after a pause are votes the court order made possible.
