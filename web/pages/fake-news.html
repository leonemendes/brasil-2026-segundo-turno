Fighting fake news: election claims checked with the official data

Two pieces going around social media say the 2026 runoff was rigged: a video with charts of the vote count, and a "forensic report" with tables. Below, each of their claims is checked with the official result of every urna, in files that anyone can download from the TSE.

In the charts, the line is Lula's share of the votes (Haddad's in 2018). When Lula's line goes up, Bolsonaro's goes down by the same amount.

1 · The video about "patterns" in the count

The video adds urnas one by one, plots the running vote share, and makes four claims.

Claim 1 · "The urnas arrive in random order"

False: the urnas arrive place by place

If the urnas arrived in random order, the line would stay inside the gray band: flat and close to the final result almost from the start. The real line, in color, starts far away and moves slowly to the result. In 2022 Lula had — after the first 1% of urnas and ended with —. Some regions send their results first, and regions vote differently. The 2018 file does not say when each urna arrived, so its dashed line uses the 2026 order.

Claim 2 · "An algorithm made this curve in 2026"

False: the old elections make the same curve

We took the order in which the 2026 urnas arrived. Then we added up the 2018 and 2022 votes of the same — polling sections in that same order. The three lines have the same shape. The 2018 and 2022 votes were counted years ago, so no program in 2026 could change them. The order of the places makes the curve, not an algorithm.

Claim 3 · "Slow rises followed by sharp drops cannot happen by chance"

False: the official file makes the same zigzag

This is Acre, in the plain order of the TSE file, with nothing changed. The line moves slowly while it adds towns that vote close to the total so far. It jumps when it reaches a town that votes very differently. That is all the zigzag is. Hover over the line to see the town.

Even pure chance makes rises and drops

Each gray line is 472 urnas picked at random and added in random order, the same number of urnas the video uses. Nobody changed anything, and every line still goes up and down. With only 472 urnas, the final share lands anywhere from — to —.

Every urna prints a signed bulletin at the polling place before it sends its result. Anyone can photograph one and compare it with the TSE file. On election day, the TSE integrity test checked paper ballots against the urna records and found no difference.

2 · The "forensic report"

A document called "Relatório Pericial Independente" uses the real TSE urna files, so most of its numbers are correct. Its conclusions come from reading those numbers the wrong way. The simplest test is to run its own method on 2022, when there was no voting extension.

Report · "Until 17:00 Flávio won, and Lula's win came only from urnas closed after 17:00"

False: the same split gives the same result in 2022, with no extension

The report splits the urnas by the close time each urna records, and that time is local time. So "closed by 17:00" holds every urna in Acre, Amazonas, Mato Grosso, Mato Grosso do Sul, Rondônia and Roraima, which close at 15:00 or 16:00 local time (17:00 in Brasília). These states lean to the right. "Closed after 17:00" holds — sections that closed a minute or two after 17:00, as urnas always do.

Report · "More than 2.5 million voters voted after the deadline"

False: closing late is not voting late

The report counts every voter in the sections that closed late. Almost all of them voted during the day: a section closes late when the last person in line votes. The urna log records the time of every vote.
