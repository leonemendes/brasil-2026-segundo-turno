9 · The extension

In a normal runoff, nearly every urna closes on time

Brazil votes in sections: one room, one electronic voting machine (urna), a few hundred voters. When a section closes, its urna prints a boletim de urna with the result and the exact open and close times.

The law lets every voter who is in line at 17:00 vote, so many urnas close a few minutes late. In the 2022 runoff only — of — sections were still open 49 minutes after the close.

On 25 October an STF order at 17:03 extended voting to 19:00. The TSE told the sections still open at 17:49. Sections that had closed did not reopen.

10 · Queue or court order

A late close does not mean late votes

The urna also keeps a log with the time of every vote. Each dot here is one real vote from one urna in Cruzeiro do Sul, Acre. The bar is the moment it closed.

Top: round 1. People in line at 17:00 kept voting with no break, then the urna closed. That is a queue, and it is legal in every election.

Bottom: the same urna in the runoff. The last vote was before 17:00. The urna stayed open until 19:00, and nobody came. Its close time says "late", but it had no late votes. Only votes after a pause are votes the court order made possible.

    Fighting fake news: election claims checked with the official data

    Two pieces going around social media say the 2026 runoff was rigged: a video with charts of the vote count, and a "forensic report" with tables. Below, each of their claims is checked with the official result of every urna, in files that anyone can download from the TSE.

    In the charts, the line is Lula's share of the votes (Haddad's in 2018). When Lula's line goes up, Bolsonaro's goes down by the same amount.

    1 · The video about "patterns" in the count

    The video adds urnas one by one, plots the running vote share, and makes four claims.

    Claim 1 · "The urnas arrive in random order"

    False: the urnas arrive place by place

    If the urnas arrived in random order, the line would stay inside the gray band: flat and close to the final result almost from the start. The real line, in color, starts far away and moves slowly to the result. In 2022 Lula had — after the first 1% of urnas and ended with —. Some regions send their results first, and regions vote differently. The 2018 file does not say when each urna arrived, so its dashed line uses the 2026 order.

    Claim 2 · "An algorithm made this curve in 2026"

    False: the old elections make the same curve

    We took the order in which the 2026 urnas arrived. Then we added up the 2018 and 2022 votes of the same — polling sections in that same order. The three lines have the same shape. The 2018 and 2022 votes were counted years ago, so no program in 2026 could change them. The order of the places makes the curve, not an algorithm.

    Claim 3 · "Slow rises followed by sharp drops cannot happen by chance"

    False: the official file makes the same zigzag

    This is Acre, in the plain order of the TSE file, with nothing changed. The line moves slowly while it adds towns that vote close to the total so far. It jumps when it reaches a town that votes very differently. That is all the zigzag is. Hover over the line to see the town.

    Even pure chance makes rises and drops

    Each gray line is 472 urnas picked at random and added in random order, the same number of urnas the video uses. Nobody changed anything, and every line still goes up and down. With only 472 urnas, the final share lands anywhere from — to —.

    Every urna prints a signed bulletin at the polling place before it sends its result. Anyone can photograph one and compare it with the TSE file. On election day, the TSE integrity test checked paper ballots against the urna records and found no difference.

    2 · The "forensic report"

    A document called "Relatório Pericial Independente" uses the real TSE urna files, so most of its numbers are correct. Its conclusions come from reading those numbers the wrong way. The simplest test is to run its own method on 2022, when there was no voting extension.

    Report · "Until 17:00 Flávio won, and Lula's win came only from urnas closed after 17:00"

    False: the same split gives the same result in 2022, with no extension

    The report splits the urnas by the close time each urna records, and that time is local time. So "closed by 17:00" holds every urna in Acre, Amazonas, Mato Grosso, Mato Grosso do Sul, Rondônia and Roraima, which close at 15:00 or 16:00 local time (17:00 in Brasília). These states lean to the right. "Closed after 17:00" holds — sections that closed a minute or two after 17:00, as urnas always do.

    Report · "More than 2.5 million voters voted after the deadline"

    False: closing late is not voting late

    The report counts every voter in the sections that closed late. Almost all of them voted during the day: a section closes late when the last person in line votes. The urna log records the time of every vote. In the urnas still open after 17:45 there were only — votes after 17:00. Even if every one of them went to Lula, his margin would stay between — and — votes.

    The report compares the late sections with the whole state. The right comparison is the place where they are. In São Paulo, — of the — late sections are in São Paulo city, where Lula is much stronger than in the rest of the state.

    How this was built

    • Round 1, 2018 and 2022: the TSE "boletim de urna" open-data files. One row per section and candidate, with the urna's open and close times. The totals match the official results exactly.
    • 2026 runoff: the TSE results service. National and state totals match the official result (Lula 64,256,232, Flávio 59,826,041). Municipal files are fetched slowly to respect the TSE's rate limit, so the map fills in over the day.
    • Close time: urnas record local time. Each municipality's official close is its usual opening hour plus 9 hours (8:00 to 17:00). In 2022 and 2026 that is 17:00 Brasília time everywhere; in 2018 it was 17:00 local time.
    • "Open 49+ minutes": the urna closed at 17:49 Brasília or later. 17:49 is when the TSE told sections still open about the extension.
    • Queue or court order: the urna log has a time for every vote, but not the candidate. Votes that continue after 17:00 with less than 5 minutes between them are the 17:00 queue. Votes after a pause of 5 minutes or more are new arrivals that only the extension allowed.
    • Abroad: the TSE gives each overseas city a code and a Portuguese name only. The city positions on the world map were added by hand and are approximate. All vote numbers are the TSE's.
    • Map shapes: IBGE municipal boundaries (tbrugz/geodata-br), simplified; world countries from Natural Earth (world-atlas). A few very new municipalities have no shape.
    • Votes after 17:00: counted from the log of every urna that closed at 17:45 Brasília or later, downloaded one by one from the TSE results service. The list of those urnas comes from the independent analysis apolinario/urnas-tardias; every count on this page is ours, made from the TSE logs, and matches that analysis except for a few votes logged at exactly 17:00:00. Urnas that closed before 17:45 were not re-downloaded; their late votes are the normal end-of-day queue.
    • Count patterns: the runoff boletim de urna files for 2018, 2022 and 2026. In 2022 and 2026 the column DT_BU_RECEBIDO gives the time the TSE received each urna; the 2018 file has no such column. The 2026-order replay uses the sections with the same state, municipality, zone and section number in all three years. Random orders use a fixed seed, so a rebuild gives the same lines. Built by scripts/count-order.ts.
    • Forensic report check: scripts/report-check.ts reads the 2022 and 2026 runoff boletim de urna files and recomputes the report's tables: the split by the close time the urna records (local time), the sections closed after 19:00, the voters released without biometrics (QT_ELEI_BIOM_SEM_HABILITACAO in 2026, QT_ELEITORES_BIOMETRIA_NH in 2022) and the near-unanimous sections. It runs the same checks on 2022, which had no extension.
    • What is still pending: the TSE's bulk boletim de urna file for the runoff, with every urna's close time. When it is out, the runoff line in the close-time chart appears and the list of late urnas can be checked against it.
